Burn the Ships:  How to trap yourself into doing what you're scared to do
=========================================================================

In 1519, Hernán Cortez led an expedition to conquer the degenerate death cult known as the Aztecs of Iberoamerica.

To accomplish that feat, Cortez had to ensure that his forces maintained no illusions of retreat or desertion.  Asking his men, politely, to not scream their way back to the ships and set sail directly away from the human sacrificing Aztecs wouldn't work.  Even ordering his men with the threat of hanging was probably futile - hanging sounds downright comfortable, compared with being skinned alive.

Cortez needed to do something that would remove escape as an option.

His solution was rather simple.

As soon as they reached land, Cortez ordered all ships burned and destroyed.  Now, in order to return home, his men had to succeed in their mission.

Few of us launch such ambitious expeditions.  But we all face fears that meet or exceed those faced by those conquistadors, whether the threat to our lives are real or imagined.

For example, success in business is dependent on building a network of people that will help you grow your enterprise.  Case in point, if you don't ask someone to buy your thing, then nobody will buy your thing.

I, personally, struggle with high anxiety when faced with anything that smacks of cold calls or new relationships.  I would rather read a book on how to build a network, than actually build that network.

Instead of taking a risk and failing, I would rather just not take the risk, and do something safe instead.

This article is about how to remove retreat as an option, and force yourself to take the risk.

## Case Study:  Phone Calls

I have a habit of not making phone calls to anybody.  Especially my family.  I've gone years not talking to my parents, siblings, and other relatives, simply because I hate talking on the telephone.

The phone calls are risky.  What if I agree to do something I don't want to do?  What if I say something offensive?  My family is pretty liberal, but I've turned, and now support Trump.  What if they find out, and no longer love me?

All of those are really empty fears, but they're fear enough to make me put off a phone call until some future date, every time.

A few months ago, I hit on a "burn the ships" plan.  Instead of scheduling a phone call on my calendar, writing it on my to-do list, or otherwise promising to myself I'd make the call, I would first send a text and set up a time with that other person.  That way, I'd at least suffer guilt at missing an appointment if I didn't make the call.
