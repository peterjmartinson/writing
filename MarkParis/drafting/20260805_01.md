As Mark sat, waiting for his two crewmates to arrive fresh from their meeting with Managment, his attention turned to a discussion between two of the lanky kids at one of the other tables.

"Where did I put that thing?"  One of the kids was searching through a formless brown sack, pulling out various featureless rectangles and setting them on the table.  He was wearing a bright orange jacket and had green hair.  He looked to Mark almost like a fresh carrot.

The other kid was already deep into whatever was happening on his terminal, massive headphones covering most of his head, hunched under an oversized dark blue sweater.

"Bromine, what are you looking for?" said the friend, sounding like he was in a bit of a trance.

"The adapter," said the ever more frantic carrot kid.

Looking up, annoyed, and slipping one cup off an ear, "What?"

"The adapter.  You know, for my headphones."

"Bromine, why don't you just get the cordless ones?"

"Sound quality is shit," said they boy on the left.  He couldn't have been more than 16 years old.  He sat back and looked helplessly at all the stuff he had heaped in front of him.  "Where did I put that thing?"

"I think it's in Uranus," quipped the friend.

Before he had slid the headphone cup back over his ear, a clear, confident voice resounded from the counter, "It's oo-RAH-nus, you idiot."

Mark glanced over near the register and saw a customer who he'd missed coming in, waiting for her coffee.

"Go back to school."

She was shorter than the barista, just about normal sized for an Earth native.  Straight black hair tied back in a pony tail, back to Mark, drumming her fingers on the glass counter.

"Bell?" called the giant barista, as she walked back to the customer with a plastic cup of coffee.

"It's Mel, and I've been standing in front of you here for like five minutes."  She grabbed her coffee, and turned to head out of the shop.  She glanced quickly at Mark on the way out, so he could gander at her less than interesting features.  All that stood out were her uneven black eyebrows and the surprising grin she appeared to be trying to hide.

She almost ran over Windy and Alejandro, who were just coming into the coffee shop.

"Well this is a tiny establishment, huh?" Windy said as she eased into one of the chairs at Mark's table.  Alejandro was at the counter ordering.  "What did I miss?"

"I guess I should be asking you that," Mark responded.  He knew he was in for some kind of demerit for the stunt he pulled out at the Lagrange 4 point.

"I'll get into that when some coffee gets into me."

"Alejandro?"  Mark looked over as the smiling barista gave Alex the two steaming cups of Joe.

"Thank you", said Alejandro.

"How did you get her to say your name right?" asked Mark when Alejandro sat down.

Alejandro looked at him for a moment, still as a snake, before explaining, "She asked what my name was, and I told her.  Alejandro Alireza Sharif al-Muktaba Schwartz Cota."
