Elsinore station had only had people on it for about 20 years.  Before that, it was only the drones.

StarMetals Corp had started building the station back in the '70s, and it came in waves.  At first, it was little more than a staging platform for the harvesting drones, that would swoop down into the Uranusian atmosphere to suck in megatons of methane.  The methane would then be transported to an orbital distillery where it would be stripped of impurities, liquified, and pumped into tanks ready for the deep space ships.

The drones pretty much made themselves back then.

After the robot explosion of the 2030's, humanity went through a bit of a freak out.  With robots doing all the manual labor, all the time, a lot of people felt useless.  The USA finally made good on its minimum-stipend-per-person and set the standard across the Earth.  People got crazy - Daytona Beach Girls Gone Wild crazy.  People died.

But it didn't last.  By 2040, with the assistance of the mediocre artificial intelligence and free energy to pursue research provided by the bots, we nailed fusion.  That immediately opened the Moon to mining and exploitation, what with all the helium-3 that's collected there through the eons.

Following fast on the footsteps of He-3 fusion came fusion propulsion.  Now, instead of drifting for years just to get to the next planet, we could accelerate and decelerate the entire way, cutting the trip to days or weeks.

The mining companies ruled the stars in those days.  The mining companies and their drones.  It cost a lot to support people - air, radiation shielding, constant supply of clean water, food.  Robots don't take dumps.  They need to be powered, they need regular maintenance, and they need replacing when they finally wear out.  And other robots can take care of all those other functions.

So when StarMetals started mining the asteroids at the outer planets, they sent hordes of robots.

Elsinore station started as a factory where drones would be built, maintained, and recycled.
