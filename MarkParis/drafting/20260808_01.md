"Mark", Windy said, "You're fired."

Mark felt his eyes melting in his head as he stared at Windy, all other things in the room dissolving into a blur.

"Wait, what?"

"Mark, look", Windy went on, "Patrick said they hired you because you blew them away in the interview.  Even though your experience was pretty spotty, they decided to take a chance on you.  But this is the third time you endangered your crew and mission by going off and doing some stupid stunt."  Windy was staring Mark in the eye, but he could tell she was sympathetic.

"But I won't do it again!  You know I wouldn't do anything to actually put you guys in danger.  I executed a tightly controlled spin on that ship.  The whole thing was automated and backtested.  If anything went wrong, Jemima would have brought it back to a stand still."  Mark was grasping.  Jemima was the autopilot, who he had to sweet talk into doing the maneuver in the first place.  One of Mark's talents, besides identifying the provenance of any coffee bean grown in the inner Solar System, was sweet talking around AI guardrails.

"You convinced that fucking autopilot you had the whole thing under control, Mark!  You know there are logs!"

Windy let out a big "whoosh" of breath, rubbed the palms of her hands on her thighs, and looked back up at Mark.  "And all for a cup of fucking coffee.  You know how bad that cockpit smelled when we got back in there?  Like a bunch of dead rotting skunks.  Look, I stood up for you too.  I told Patrick we can't do proper maintenance just Alex and me, and it's not exactly easy to just hire another guy out here.  But he said no.  You're out."

Alejandro had just gotten back with the two coffees.  Mark noticed a nice, clear "Alejandro" written on his cup.  As usual, his face was as expressionless as a cat's.  He looked at Windy, who nodded at him.  Alejandro looked back at Mark and put his hand on Mark's shoulder.

"I'm sorry, hermano."

Mark wasn't sure what to do.  It's one thing to get fired at a fix it shop on Earth.  It's another to get fired orbiting one of the outer planets.

"Will I still get paid for the work?"

Windy nodded, "yeah, you'll still get your last paycheck.  Which is good, because we're not authorized to let you on the ship when we undock."

"Wait," said Mark, "you're not taking me back?"

"Nope.  You're going to have to pay for a ride back with someone else.  Compliance says we can't fly passengers between destinations for safety reasons."

Mark's head was spinning.  He'd been laid off before, back when he was a generator tech on Mars.  That wasn't such a big deal, because there was a ton of work to do there, and there were ferries going back to Earth all the time.  Not many people wanted to come out here.

Uranus was kind of an outpost, like Green River, Wyoming, back in the old west of America.  There weren't many rides back.  Most ships headed further out, for mining Triton or the Kuiper Belt objects.  Supply rigs could come out every few months with stuff you couldn't get out deep, like heavy metals, pharmaceuticals, agricultural micronutrients.  But they were usually drone craft that didn't host passengers very comfortably.

"What am I going to do?"

Alejandro gave his shoulder a squeeze and a little shake.  "You will be OK, hermano.  You are smart, and can fix things.  Also," he looked at the cup in his hand and back at Mark, "the coffee is quite good."

"We'll be here for another day or so while the ship gets overhauled, so you can come grab your things whenever," Windy said.

They talked some more, but Mark couldn't really process any more of the discussion.

Stuck on Elsinore Base until who knows when.  Well, this sucks.
